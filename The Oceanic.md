## Biology

The Oceanic is a variant of the Slugcat species, being one of *many*. What sets the Oceanic apart is its ability to thrive both on land and in the oceans indefinitely. Its biology is unique from other slugcats, and this versatility across two completely different biomes gives it an advantage not many other slugcats possess.

### Habitat

The Oceanic resides in the deep oceans beyond the retaining wall and the vast lands above. Although built for the seas, it ventures onto land frequently, primarily because the land provides more diverse types of food.

### Appearance

Its skin is colored in a gradient of teals and deep navies, tapering into a pale cream underside. Spots of bioluminescence are scattered along its back down to its tail. Its four eyes grant excellent vision in both darkness and water, and its gills, located on the sides of the head, are deep lavender and can sometimes shift to a pink or blue depending on the situation. 

Webbed paws and a tail with fin-like webbing on both sides provide lift and agility in swimming. On each paw, a venomous spur resides just below the lower joint where the fingers connect to the paw. This spur delivers a potent hallucinogenic toxin capable of incapacitating attackers when administered. On the base of each finger lies a grip pad, mainly designed for traction on slippery surfaces in or around water, but is also greatly utilized on normal land.

### Diet

The Oceanic is omnivorous. In the ocean, it feeds on small fish, underwater plant life, and occasionally larger aquatic creatures. Since food can be sparse and hunting can be dangerous in deep waters, it often forages on land. Here, its diet includes fruit, popcorn plant, batflies, and basically anything it can catch, kill, or gather.

**⦿⦿⦿⦿⦿|〇〇〇**

The Oceanic requires 5 food pips to hibernate and has 3 pips to carry over.

### Reproduction

The Oceanic reproduces asexually via budding. When it starts the process, a bud forms on the top side of the tail. In ~1–2 cycles, the bud detaches and rapidly begins to internally separate, developing a juvenile inside. The egg has faint bioluminescence under the blue outer membrane, helping the parent locate it and signaling that it is still healthy.

This bioluminescence also plays a role in genetic diversity. During the formation, ATP hydrolysis reactions can sometimes leak water into the DNA of the developing egg, causing subtle but stable mutations in the process. That very same DNA is copied across all cells, leading to that mutation to stick. Within ~2–3 cycles, the Oceanic slugpup emerges.

After hatching, the parent promptly leaves the slugpup to mature independently. For the first few cycles, the slugpup consumes microscopic plankton and algae while keeping far away from danger. Once plankton and algae are no longer sufficient, the juvenile learns to hunt and forage for more substantial food. The parent continues its life with little to no contact with its offspring until ascension. Usually the Oceanic only produces 1 or 2 pups per lifespan.

### Behavior

The Oceanic is not a very social creature, especially compared to other slugcats. This is likely due to the lack of parental care, which reduces the need for social interaction. Highly independent like most slugcats, it prefers to hunt and scavenge alone.

When hunting, hiding, or in a hostile location, the Oceanic minimizes the use of its bioluminescence to avoid unwanted attention. On land, it will attempt to neutralize threats through conventional means, such as a spear or piece of rebar. If that fails, or if it is underwater, it relies on its venomous spurs to incapacitate the foe. Depending on hunger levels, it may inject the predator with more toxin and consume it, or leave it alive with only a survivable amount of venom and flee.

If the Oceanic can avoid killing a creature when not required, they will. They don't want to cause death when unwarranted (e.g., hunger, self-preservation).

>Even the survivable dose of venom still induces intense and vivid hallucinations for a few minutes in the recipient.

## Physiology

### Egg Propagation

When undergoing reproduction, the Oceanic releases the hormone **Noetrophin-a1** throughout the body, which eventually binds to the **Luminal Morphogenetic c2 Receptor a1 (LMc2Ra1)** receptors in the budding organ within its tail. Activation of **LMc2Ra1** starts a morphogenetic signaling cascade, stimulating DNA replication, cytoplasmic reorganization, and controlled cellular differentiation within the forming bud. Once the genetic nucleus is established, it's enclosed in a protective membrane. Rapid cell division then begins, shaping the egg. Finally, specialized bioluminescent photocyte precursors are activate, leading to a faint bioluminescent glow.

As mentioned in the **reproduction** section, the bioluminescence helps maintain genetic diversity. During the formation of the photocyte precursors, ATP hydrolysis slightly elevates the local concentration of reactive water molecules, increasing the likelihood of **hydrolytic DNA damage** in the developing genetic core. These events produce small, stable mutations in genes controlling morphology, pigmentation, and body proportions, contributing to genetic diversity.

```text
Phototropin-a1 --> Budding organ activated --> ATP Hydrolysis --> Bioluminescence
                                               ↳ Hydrolytic DNA Damage
                             + Replication Errors --> ↳ Possible Stable Mutations
```

Mutations also occur naturally during cell replication, when occasional DNA polymerase errors escape repair. The combination of **environmental hydrolysis** and **intrinsic replication variance** ensures that each slugpup exhibits subtle yet stable genetic differences, maintaining the Oceanic’s adaptive variability across generations.

### Bioluminescence

The Oceanic’s bioluminescence is produced by a light emitting molecule called **Mariluciferin**, which is activated by the enzyme **Cerulophotoxidase**.

```text
Mariluciferin + O2 --[Cerulophotoxidase + Ca2]--> Light + Oxidized Mariluciferin
Oxidized Mariluciferin + ATP --[Mariphotoreductase]--> Mariluciferin
```

When the Oceanic sends oxygen to **Cerulophotoxidase**, **Mariluciferin** oxidizes, producing a soft teal and navy glow. Specialized photocyte cells produce **Mariphotoreductase**, rapidly restoring **oxidized Mariluciferin** to its active form, allowing perpetual illumination without depleting chemical reserves.

### Venom Biochemistry

The Oceanic delivers a potent venom, **Thalasocyanin**, via a hollow spur on each paw. Thalasocyanin is a **trimeric complex** consisting of a small lipophilic alkaloid (**Cyanoliphin**), a disulfide-rich neuropeptide (**Duracyanethrin**), and an enzymatic carrier protein (**Thalase-c1**). The complex is stored in subdermal granules and released by muscular pressure on the spur.

```text
(50% dose)

Venom Injected
       ↓
Cyanoliphin [small lipophilic alkaloid] → Central perceptual disruption (hallucinations)
Duracyanethrin [disulfide-rich peptide] → Local neuromuscular block + vasodilation
       ↓
Metabolism & clearance
```

**Onset and dose:** Cyanoliphin is a small (~200–500 Da), highly lipophilic tertiary amine that easily crosses the blood brain barrier. At high local doses and in small animals, it can cause perceptual distortion within seconds, while in larger animals, onset may require tens of seconds to minutes depending on vascular uptake. Duracyanethrin produces local flaccid paralysis within seconds at the site of injection and can progress to systemic paralysis over tens of seconds to minutes depending on total dose and target size.

#### Composition

```text
Thalasocyanin = {Cyanoliphin + Duracyanethrin + Thalase-c1}
```

As stated above, **Thalasocyanin** is not a single compound. It consists of **three** primary components:

1. **Cyanoliphin:** a tryptamine-like compound that mainly acts as an agonist at **serotonin (5-HT2A)** receptors, producing vivid hallucinations, and lightly acts as an antagonist at **NMDA** receptors, contributing to perceptual disorientation and altered sensory processing.

2. **Duracyanethrin:** a small, disulfide-stabilized peptide that blocks **nicotinic acetylcholine receptors** at the muscles, stopping nerve signals and causing quick paralysis. It also It induces vasodilation via endothelial **nitric oxide (NO)** release, relaxing vessel walls, and slightly triggers histamine release which enhances systemic venom distribution.

3. **Thalase-c1:** a calcium-binding enzyme-carrier protein that stabilizes Cyanoliphin and Duracyanethrin in the body and in water, while reducing local immune responses long enough for the venom to act.

**Delivery mechanics:** the venom is stored in subdermal granules at acidic pH. Pressure on the spur forces granule contents through the spur duct. The change in pH and salt balance activate **Thalase-c1**, exposing the active sites of Cyanoliphin and Duracyanethrin. **Thalase-c1** also reduces neutrophil recruitment and binds complement proteins, slowing mass immune detection and local inflammation so the venom stays active long enough to work.

**Duration & clearance:** At typical environmental temperatures, the venom’s effects last several minutes (**2–10 min**). **Thalase-c1** then promotes enzymatic breakdown while the body metabolizes the compounds. Outside the body, **Cyanoliphin** is unstable in air, losing potency within seconds to minutes. In water, however, the hydrophobic **Cyanoliphin** is encapsulated by **Thalase-c1**, which protects it from hydrolysis and oxidation, allowing the venom to remain active for up to **4-8 minutes**.

#### Biochemical Construction

**Thalasocyanin** is biosynthesized in the Oceanic’s paired venom glands as three parallel components: the lipophilic alkaloid **Cyanoliphin**, the disulfide-rich peptide **Duracyanethrin**, and the calcium-binding carrier protein **Thalase-c1**. Cyanoliphin is derived from tryptophan through sequential decarboxylation, hydroxylation, methylation, and lipid conjugation, producing a small CNS-active molecule. Duracyanethrin is synthesized as a ribosomal prepropeptide, folded and stabilized by disulfide bonds and post-translational modifications. Thalase-c1 folds into a hydrophobic pocket that binds Cyanoliphin and coordinates Duracyanethrin, forming an inactive pro-complex.

The components are packaged into acidic granules, where Thalase-c1 stabilizes them and shields the complex from immune detection. Upon spur activation, pH and ionic shifts trigger Thalase-c1 conformational opening and proteolytic cleavage of Duracyanethrin proregions, releasing the active toxin into tissue and blood. The venom is enzymatically cleared within minutes, while Cyanoliphin remains transiently active in aqueous environments due to hydrophobic encapsulation.

Biosynthesis
   │
   ├─ Cyanoliphin (alkaloid) <-- tryptophan modification --> lipophilic
   ├─ Duracyanethrin (prepropeptide) <-- folding + disulfide bond formation

### Respiration

The Oceanic’s respiratory system consists of two pairs of **gill filaments** and **internal lungs**, which switch priority depending on the environment.

When underwater, the Oceanic uses its gills. They are covered in small blood vessels, which when submerged, extract oxygen from the water and dispose of carbon dioxide. Their structure is similar to that of its sister species (The Rivulet), but the Oceanic’s gills are far more efficient, allowing it to stay submerged indefinitely.

On land, the internal lungs are used instead of the gills. These lungs draw in air, extracting oxygen while releasing carbon dioxide and other byproducts. The lungs extract oxygen efficiently, allowing the Oceanic to spend time on land indefinitely. The lungs can also be used as a swim bladder, inflating and deflating with oxygen to float or sink. This is also what's used to keep the Oceanic neutrally buoyant when underwater.

### Visual System

The Oceanic has a **tetrad of eyes**: an upper pair, of compressed ovals and a lower pair of shorter, more compressed ovals. This difference in shape and placement is for specialization in two different environments. Each set contains a special lens that can slightly shift forwards or backwards to help maintain focus across differing refractive indices between air and water.

**Upper eyes:** Optimized for visualization in air and above the waterline. Their taller, less compressed lenses allow for better focusing across variable refractive indices and improved vertical perception. Although adapted for land, they are still used underwater in tandem with the lower pair, reducing blind spots and allowing for better vision in more complicated terrain.

**Lower eyes:** Specialized in underwater vision. The slightly more flattened shape increases horizontal field and reduces spherical aberration in water allowing for a clearer picture and improved edge detection. These eyes have a denser rod population to improve light sensitivity in dim, blue-shifted light. While still functional on land, their primary role is to help the upper pair by filling peripheral blind zones and allowing better vision in low light environments.

#### Neural Integration and Switching
The brain integrates input from all four eyes into a composite visual map. Priority shifts depending on environment (water or land). When submerged, the lower pair dominates contrast and edge detection. On land, the upper pair leads in focus and detail. This switching is fast and mostly subconscious, only causing brief blurriness during adjustment.

### Hemerythrin

Oceanic gets its blood color from **Hemerythrin**.

**Hemerythrin** is a non-heme iron-based oxygen carrier protein. Unlike hemoglobin which uses a porphyrin ring, Hemerythrin binds oxygen directly to two iron atoms. In its oxygenated state, it produces a violet-pink. Deoxygenated state is colorless to pale yellow. The Oceanic's blood is a purple color because it's almost always partially oxygenated.

Because the Oceanic moves between water and land constantly, oxygenation state fluctuates. Blood is more deeply violet after surfacing from a dive. Slightly paler after extended time on land. This is visible in wounds or through the thin skin near the gills, or the gills themselves.

```text
Hemerythrin + O2 --> Oxyhemerythrin (violet-pink)
Oxyhemerythrin --> Hemerythrin - O2 (colorless/pale yellow)
```

The color shifts described in the gills vasculature system are a direct result of Hemerythrin's oxygenation state responding to environmental changes. More deeply violet when oxygen-rich, shifting toward pale pink as levels drop.

## Personality

>NOTE: May change!!!

Being a very solitary creature, the Oceanic doesn’t understand companionship. Surviving alone and learning by themselves all their life left them wandering constantly, physically and mentally. But beneath that confusion, they want to know what it’s like. What it’s like to have help, to not be seen as food or dangerous, **to be understood**. They don’t like that desire; they’re too scared to act on it, but they can’t suppress it either.

Fearing the unknown in a world filled with unknowns is exhausting. A need to *know* what everything is and *how* it runs. The unfamiliar and non-constant are deeply terrifying things... The Oceanic trusts patterns more than instinct, mind more than gut. In their view, everything is temporary. From shelter and safety, to even light and food, nothing lasts forever. They don't rush unless necessary. They don't act without thinking. To them, hesitation means death.

Curiosity covers over the deep void left by their solitude. Thinking and learning is what keeps them from needing anything else, or *anyone* else. They run, hide, and suppress their deeper emotions because to them it's just noise. Patterns are quiet, logical, safe. Emotions can be sporadic, **loud**, and a part of something greater. But sometimes, something breaks through: a weed in the clearing, a feeling too strong to ignore, and they're deeply terrified of it. Usually they try to analyze it, fit it into something that can be named. But feelings can't be grasped like they'd want.

To them, the world is like water, always shifting, never the same twice. Nothing lasts, not light, not the calm, not even the things they love. Because of this view, they've stopped loving anything too deeply, since it was easier that way. They keep everything they can't understand buried so deep, that to them, they don't even exist. But it scratches, it claws, it **yearns** to be free. A cloud can only hold so much water before it starts to fall...

The Oceanic's world is exhausting to them, never a pause in the downpour of thoughts. But not thinking hurts too much to bare. Even in safety and peace, the drip never stops, the thoughts of how things work. What would the name of this be...? What's this thing...? What's that thing...? *What would it feel like to not be **alone***

#### Timeline Context

The story of the Oceanic begins **between the Rivulet and Saint campaigns**, during a period when unusually heavy rainfall caused the formation of a vast ocean. Around this time, **Five Pebbles** and **the Rot** were at peak activity, producing more water than could be cycled back into the superstructure. Additional failures in nearby iterators, including those near the retaining wall of LTTM and FP, caused water to accumulate in previously dry areas, contributing to the formation of **The Basin**.

#### The Basin

Geologically, **the Basin** is classified as a lake. However, because of its immense size and biodiversity (in certain areas), it's essentially an ocean. The Basin's fauna and flora is comprised but not limited to: kelp, algae, coral, multiple species of small and medium fish, stingrays, sharks, and much more. However, the biodiversity of this region is densely clustered in patches and sparse in most other places.

However, the biodiversity of this region is densely clustered in patches and sparse in most other places. For instance, the **Abyss**, where light cannot reach, remains mostly barren. The Abyss has the major benefit of being safe from the rain, as it cannot pierce deep enough to reach it. However, the lack of light severely limits the fauna and flora in this region, which makes up most of the Basin. 

Of course, life always finds a way. At the depths of the Basin, where the earth fissures and cracks, some creatures are said to have learned to survive off the void fluid, in a similar way the ancients once did. But that’s just a myth, and likely not to be true.

The main biome with the most biodiversity are the **Shelves**, which are scattered all throughout the Basin. The Shelfs sit at the hit depth for rainfall impact, so most life hides beneath rock formations, overhangs, or other geological structures that can shield anything below.

- **Abyss:** A deep region where sunlight cannot penetrate. Sparse life survives here, but it is shielded from rainfall, providing safety from environmental disturbances.

- **Shelves:** Scattered throughout the Basin, these shallow, rocky regions are the richest in biodiversity. They provide shelter and resources for aquatic life, protecting them from rainfall and predators.

### Prologue

"Oh, the pain of interaction!" Red-stained water caught a riptide. "Oh, the pain of trying to forgive!" Water saturated with deep red. A sense of panic flowed through the water. A voice tried to cry out, but was replaced with a deafening crash. "I'm-" another loud crash interrupted it, and everything went black.

Colors swirled behind four eyes. Hundreds of thoughts filled every neuron, memories forcing themselves forward against its recipient's will. *Oh how this went badly. I'm sorry.* Every nerve ending flooded with pain and guilt, every paw and limb numb. The taste and smell of metal flooded the mouth and nose.

Pained eyes blinked open. A beaten body tried to get up. An apology tried to be formed but was shot down by another deafening crash.

"Is it betrayal if it's truly your fault?!"

A fourth crash flooded every nerve again. Too weak to form words, the water stained red. *This is what it all led to. What a coincidence.* The barrage of crashes continued, with not even a chance at negotiation. That very chance was lost last cycle.

And then silence. Not peace, not forgiveness, not mercy. Just the feeling of a heartbeat through every limb. The currents washed away the crimson red water, leaving only guilt.

***I'm sorry.***

### Chapter 1 -- Monochromatica

Oceanic wandered into what seemed to be a ruin of some kind. Filled with too many pipes and openings, they found themselves lost, resurfacing in an air pocket to think. The air was thick with something they didn't want to breathe in. Their ears twitched as they looked at the small ripples on the water's surface illuminated by their faint glow. They dipped back down and looked at the options they had, and chose a random pipe. The faint glow of their spots was the only thing cutting through the dark waters.
 
After what seemed like an endless maze of pipes, they saw a faint beam of light coming from a crack in the wall. They went into another pipe, which this time led to a hallway with small cracks in the roof letting more sunlight beam in. They kept swimming, eventually finding a pipe that brought them to a more cramped hallway with more light shimmering throughout. After entering the pipe at the end of this hallway, they were spat out into a vast coral reef like none they had seen before.
 
Fish swam by, above and below. They looked around for a shelter since the rain was nearing and this place was far too shallow to survive out in the open. They started swimming right... *no time for sightseeing*. While passing by and grabbing a fish, they noticed a shelter hidden behind a large growth of kelp. They ate fast. *The rain isn't far.* Speeding towards the shelter, they got in. The loud sounds of the water draining from the shelter filled the small area. Oceanic sat down as they faintly heard the rain stir up the water. They realized they had no idea where they were or how to get back.
 
Water rushed into the shelter as the door began to open, slamming Oceanic into the wall until the pressure was finally even. They shook it off and swam out of the shelter, now getting a good look at the coral reef they were in. The coral grew beneath large rock formations, sheltered from the rain. There were families of fish and stingrays swimming and floating around. It was much more alive than where Oceanic was used to.
 
They looked up at the faint surface above. They swam up to try and look at where in the Basin they were located. Just as they were about to pass the cliffs circling the reef, a giant creature swam by in a flash, pushing Oceanic back. It was too fast to see... they looked around, trying to find it but to no avail. They tried to swim up again but it rushed by once more. This time they were ready, and stabbed it with the spur on their right paw. The creature immediately stopped and then started swimming sporadically in circles.
 
They finally got a good look at it. It looked like a stingray, but with a strange dorsal fin on the top and bottom. They decided to leave it be and swam to the surface. Popping their head above and shaking the water off their face, they saw endless ocean as far as they could see. They were a bit confused how a small journey to look at a ruin they passed by somehow led them to the middle of the Basin. They plunged back down below to look at their surroundings. They decided to swim back and try to go back into the pipe they came from. As they approached the location, they saw it was an empty cliff face... Like there was nothing there to begin with.
 
They went right, trying to get behind the cliff. After a bit of swimming they found a cave opening along the cliff wall and decided to go in. They kept swimming through the dark cave, which was faintly lit up by their spots. At the very end of this long cave was a single shelter and a patch of mushrooms. They knew these mushrooms were safe to eat, so at least they had food for this cycle. They gobbled up the mushrooms and entered the shelter, prompting it to loudly drain the water kept within. *They swear it gets louder every time...*

### Chapter 2 -- Under The Sand

Water rushed in, awakening Oceanic from a deeper sleep than usual. Fully awakened when they were slammed to the wall from the pressure. Swimming out of the shelter, they almost forgot they were in a cave, expecting a view of the coral. Swimming through the tight cavern for what seemed like too long, they eventually reached the open water. Deciding to swim above the cliff, they saw it stretched much further than expected. Swimming across, they watched as groups of fish darted underneath them. Quite a while passed, swimming across rocky surface with little plant life living on it. After a while, they started to get a better look at the edge... it was just sand. Whiter than usual, but sand nonetheless.
 
After a bit, they reached the edge. They swam down and looked at the cliff behind them. It was just a smoothed slope with coral growing near the bottom. They turned around, looking around the new area. *Sand... all just sand... as far as they could see.* Not a single rock, cliff, plant, or even fish in sight. Only the vast cliff behind them and an empty flat of cratered sand shaped by the rainfall. They swam into the clearing hesitantly, almost not wanting to. It was too empty, too clear. They stopped and swam a bit lower, grabbing a pawful of the sand, staring at it. *Why would there be a giant patch of this... with nothing in it?*
 
Swimming again, they saw something in the distance, but it was too far to get a proper look at it. Swimming faster out of curiosity, it started to come into focus: a pillar of some unknown material stuck in the sand. Getting closer, they saw it had markings on it, markings they recognized but didn't understand. Stopping in front of it, they saw it was about double their size, 3 markings on each of its 4 sides.
 
After a moment, they recognized them: the symbols the ancients used that they saw everywhere on the surface. They laid a paw on it carefully. Strangely cold. *Why would there just be a pillar out here?*
 
Circling around it, there was nothing special to it. It was just a pillar with markings on it stuck in the sand. They dug underneath the pillar, just to be sure. As they dug, they felt something weird underneath the sand: a black... flesh? They jumped back in surprise, their spots flashing a bit as they did.
 
After calming down from the scare, they gave it a poke... nothing. They gave it another more firm poke... nothing. Whatever it was, it seemed to be dead. They started to dig out more around it, hoping to identify it. After some time, a substantial amount around it had been dug up. It seemed to be made up of fleshy bulbs or... growths all stuck together, black in color. A tendril connected to it seemed to span further into the sand, and too deep for them to dig it out in a reasonable amount of time.
 
*It doesn't look like it's from the Basin... how would something from the land get out here... underneath a pillar... in the middle of nowhere?*
 
They swam up to the surface, popping their head above the water's surface, looking around. Still no land to be seen... wait, no... there was a line on the horizon... Land! But what side? The familiar or unknown? They dipped back down, looking down at the partially dug out creature next to the pillar, and the vast emptiness. After a moment, they decided to travel to the land. Swimming towards the direction of the landmass.
 
Time passed. Nothing but water and their own thoughts. In the midst of this, a worry crossed their mind: *What if the rain comes before I find a shelter...?* They brushed the thought aside, and kept swimming, a bit faster now. Swimming quietly for a bit, they were left to think about what they found this cycle. *Where am I... what is the pillar for... what could that creature be? It couldn't be a fish... it doesn't look like a lizard or a vulture... I swear I've seen something like it before, I had to... but where... where did I see something like that...*
 
They were brought out of their thoughts as they noticed the sky start to darken, clouds covering the sky. As the first drops of rain shot into the water around them, they spotted something. A small cliff with coral underneath it. a possible, if they were lucky, shelter! They swam as fast as they could... *I'm not going to make it*... they felt the water starting to push them around. *I'm not going to make it*... lifted sand started to block their vision. *I'm not going to make it*.
 
Suddenly, through the sand impairing their vision, they saw a pipe in the middle of the sand. Stopping quickly and taking a glance, *a shelter pipe!* A drop of water shot beside them, almost slamming into Oceanic. They raced into it as a spear of rain almost hit them again. They dropped in and floated down to the floor, letting out a big sigh of relief. They thought they were dead for sure. Water draining from the shelter loudly, they laid their head down, staring at the shelter's bland roof, still full of adrenaline. The adrenaline faded. Thoughts slowed. They were almost asleep when it hit them. **Food.**

### Chapter 3 -- When Silence Breaks

The shelter filled with water. Not yet fully awake and too weak to push back, they were thrashed around by the rushing water. As the pressure stabilized, their eyes blinked open. Slowly crawling through the shelter pipe, they were suddenly blinded by the white sand and the sun reflecting off it. It was far too bright. Their stomach rumbled, reminding them of their hunger.
 
They swam towards the coral fields they spotted last cycle. Exhaustion crept into every limb until each stroke felt painful. Pushing through the exhaustion, they kept swimming, trying to make good time. Eventually it was too much, laying down on the warm sand to rest for a bit. *The coral looks impossibly far from here... Probably just the tiredness playing tricks.* Once they moved, the barren stretch ended. A lone fish drifted past and put up almost no struggle.
 
*Dumb fish.*
 
The quick meal filled them enough to push deeper into the coral, snatching more fish where they could. Not the best fish they'd had, but it would have to do for now. Looking up at the water above, they were reminded about their finding last cycle. Once surfacing, they spotted the landmass again. Still distant, but maybe reachable in two or three cycles? Dipping back under, they swam to the sea floor and resumed the long journey towards land. The cycle blurred into the mindless task of swimming, nothing but coral and their own thoughts. *... the pillar. The black thing underneath it. What... what... what?*
 
Motion out of the corner of their eye caught their attention, a jetfish swimming in circles. *Jetfish usually are in the colder waters, strange that it's all the way out here. Not complaining.* They grabbed onto its back and steered it towards land, hoping that their destination would bring them closer to figuring out where they'd ended up.
 
Water rushed past as the jetfish wove between cliffs and coral shelves. Eventually the vast field of coral ended and they reached a large drop. *Black, all the way down. Must lead down to the Abyss...* Looking into the depths, all that was there were the floating minerals shifting and reacting as they sped past. They steered the jetfish up, shooting it out of the water. The landmass ahead was littered with machinery and architecture, though still too far away to make out the finer details.
 
As the jetfish smashed back into the water, Oceanic lost their grip. Their arms slipped and the jetfish sped away too fast to catch up to. As the water settled, everything felt too still. Floating in the slightly chilly water silently for a moment, they regretfully looked down. Their tail shifted and tensed beneath them. Nothing but black depths below. The creeping fear of the cycle ending pressed further.
 
*Stupid jetfish...*
 
They hung there for a moment. Then started swimming down into the darker waters. They could only hope there was a shelter nearby. The alternative was the Abyss. Getting darker. And darker. And darker. They finally reached some type of bottom. *Not the Abyss yet. Still some light. Maybe a shelter nearby.*
 
A faint red glow to their left caught their attention. *An Angler.* Their heartbeat rose a bit as the glow got a bit closer. *They shouldn't be this far up... maybe it got lost? Luckily, they were far enough away that it wouldn't charge... probably.* The reminder that they weren't alone sent a shiver down their spine and urged them to get to a shelter quicker.
 
They eventually hit a cliff face and there was a shelter on it. *Getting awfully lucky with shelters recently...* Racing towards it, fearing that the cycle's end was near, they shot inside. They slapped down onto the shelter floor, which seemed to be already fully drained. *Weird...* The water brought in drained away quickly. They pushed themselves up off the floor, confused and disoriented, their eyes blinked open again. Suddenly, as they were getting up, a voice came from the corner of the shelter.
 
"...You okay?"
 
Oceanic scrambled backwards to the opposite corner. *I must have imagined that, right?* They looked up, seeing another slugcat in the shelter. Their tail curled around them, their body tensing up. *This slugcat oddly enough didn't have gills... well, ones that they could see at least. His... fur? was a dark greenish... he didn't look aquatic at all... let alone even being able to be this deep down.*
 
"I didn't mean to scare you." The slugcat let out a soft giggle as he realized he accidentally startled this new face. "Are you sleeping here too?"
 
Oceanic didn't respond, simply staring in pure surprise that there was another slugcat right in front of them. *Another slugcat. And he seemed...*
*...friendly?*
*That couldn't be right. Every other one had been— No. It couldn't be right.*
 
"Not much of a talker? That's alright." The slugcat adjusted himself. "My name's Yarrow, by the way." A long silence filled the air for an awkward amount of time.
 
Their tail shifted a bit. *What harm can giving a name do...* "...Oceanic."
 
"Nice to meet you-" Yarrow tried to reach a paw forward and get a bit closer to Oceanic to properly greet them, but as he did, Oceanic pressed further into the corner. Yarrow retracted and sat in silence, slightly confused.
 
Oceanic loosened up a bit as he moved away. For a moment they thought that he was going to do something... *maybe they were friendly?* "Nice to meet you too..." The two sat quietly in their corners for a bit. Soon, the first muffled sound of rain started to hit outside the shelter.
 
"Maybe we should sleep." Yarrow started to lay down, getting comfortable, but still trying to stay in his corner, as to not scare Oceanic. He let out a quiet sigh. Was he a little too forward?
 
Oceanic followed suit, laying down. They weren't getting out of this shelter anytime soon, so they were going to have to get comfortable. *A new friend? Maybe. But... is it worth risking another incident? What if they don't want to be? What if I mess it up?* Too many questions. They let sleep take them instead.

### Chapter 4 -- What Pulls You Closer
 
Water sprayed Oceanic in the face as the shelter door opened, waking them up. Moments after, water violently filled the shelter. *It's stronger down here than up there.* As the water filled, Oceanic's eyes blinked open, watching as Yarrow took a deep breath and darted outside the shelter. Their body jolted out of slight panic. *Almost forgot there was another slugcat in there.* Confused by why they left so fast and, oddly, disappointed, Oceanic watched the shelter pipe for a moment, hesitating.
 
Tail shifting some, they got up and rushed outside the shelter to see what was going on. They watched as Yarrow swam up into the deep blue above. Watching as he swam away, they panicked and followed. *Bad idea bad idea bad idea.* They followed at a distance to not make it obvious. As the two swam higher and higher, more and more sunlight started to scatter into the water. Eventually, the two reached the surface, but Oceanic didn't surface immediately, instead watching what Yarrow did.
 
As soon as Yarrow surfaced, he caught his breath. *Big lung capacity, but not nearly enough for down there. Definitely not doing that again.* He noticed Oceanic lurking below. Dipping down, he signaled for Oceanic to surface.
 
Staring at Yarrow, they contemplated whether to surface. Eventually they decided to surface, popping their head above the water. A long stillness followed. *I have to say something.* Their head lowered a bit more into the water. "...You don't look aquatic."
 
"You're right!" He let out a small giggle. "I'm not. However, you DO look aquatic."
 
Oceanic watched the way he bobbed in the water. "Why are you in the middle of the Basin...?" *Maybe he got lost? Maybe he's looking for something here? Maybe—*
 
"We aren't in the middle, we are near land." Waving over at the landmass not too far away, Yarrow let out a small giggle. "I got sucked out here by a current and had to find a shelter."
 
Oceanic's gills lowered a bit. *An exaggeration, obviously.* Tension flooded the conversation and Oceanic blew some bubbles in the water.
 
As the tension grew, Yarrow got a bit impatient. "I better keep trying to head back to land. Bye!" Yarrow began swimming to the landmass ahead, leaving Oceanic bobbing on the water's surface.
 
Oceanic's gills twitched slightly and their tail curled. They lifted their head out of the water. *Why are they leaving already?* They considered following for a moment. *I can't make the same mistake as all those cycles ago...—*
 
But...
 
With a jerk of their tail they swam towards Yarrow. *Just to talk a bit longer...* They tried to catch up with him, which wasn't much of a challenge — Yarrow swam slowly. "Wait!"
 
Yarrow stopped and faced Oceanic, his head tilting to the side a bit, ears falling over.
 
"Where are you going... on land... I need to go there too." Oceanic drifted a bit closer subconsciously.
 
"I live there. I want to get back."
 
"...Can I come?" *Maybe he can make the journey easier... then I can find my way back. Back to my... home.* "I uhm... I live there too."
 
Yarrow's head tilted to the side again. "You live on land? ...I guess it does make sense, you're breathing this air and all," he gestured to the air around him and then let out a small giggle. "Sure, you can come. I don't mind."
 
"Thank you!" A sense of relief washed over Oceanic. *Finally met a slugcat that's friendly. Well... for now, at least.*
 
"Let's start actually making progress towards land. We don't have forever to float here and talk!" Yarrow started swimming again and gestured back for Oceanic to follow. He looked back to make sure they were following.
 
The two slugcats swam across the surface of the water for a while, trying to make good distance. Oceanic dipped beneath the water every now and then, watching the fish drift through the dark depths. Nothing interesting… Just the same endless blue as before.
 
Looking back up at the landmass, it was much closer. *We might reach it this cycle.* Another quick dip revealed only a steep slope falling into the deep depths. *Well that's boring…* Distracted by the blandness below, they suddenly crashed into Yarrow. *He stopped swimming for some reason.* "Ow- Sorry-"
 
"It's fine." Yarrow pointed ahead towards a building. "There's a shelter and some food in there." Yarrow started swimming again and reached land. He crawled up onto the rock-littered beach, shaking off any water left on his fur. "It's good to be back on solid ground!" He stretched a bit then started heading towards the building.
 
Oceanic followed shortly after, surfacing. *Haven't been on land in quite a bit...* Shaking any loose water off of their smooth body, they followed Yarrow. Looking at the sight up close, this looked quite different from the land they frequently went to many cycles ago... *This side was much denser, littered with pipes, wires, and buildings. Almost claustrophobic...* In the distance, a large structure was masked by thick fog. *The air was more metallic here. Less crisp.*
 
They were snapped out of thought as Yarrow entered the nearest building, going into a pipe that led to a large opening. There was a shelter on the rightmost wall. Blue fruit hung from the ceiling to their left, which Yarrow picked, handing a few to Oceanic.
 
"Sorry it's not fish or something, but it will have to do."
 
Oceanic ate the blue fruit quickly. *One of the things up here they'd missed.* "I don't just eat fish..." Their gills lowered slightly.
 
"Well, that's good!" Yarrow said, excited. "I worried that I would have needed to carry fish for you to eat."
 
Yarrow picked a few more and handed some to Oceanic to make sure they were full. Oceanic ate the rest, wiping the juice off their face. After Yarrow finished eating, they headed towards the shelter and they both got in, moving to opposite corners like last time.
 
"...Thanks for the food," they said, realizing they never thanked him.
 
"You're welcome, Oceanic." Yarrow tilted his head happily towards them and started to settle into sleep.
 
Oceanic laid down as well, letting out a small sigh of relief. *Nice not hearing the shelter drain every time... Don't miss that whatsoever. This cycle has been a good one...* Soon they both drifted to sleep.

### Chapter 5 -- Too Close

Woken by the loud sound of the shelter door opening, Oceanic jolted awake. They jumped a bit seeing Yarrow in the shelter, forgetting for a moment that anyone else was there. Looking back at Yarrow, they noticed he was already awake.

"Good morning." Yarrow said with a smile.

"... Good morning." *A warm feeling spread through them. It's strange...*

Yarrow got up, getting into the shelter's pipe. Oceanic soon followed. Blinded by the bright sun, they blinked a bit as their eyes adjusted.

"We should probably find food before we do much!" Yarrow started walking deeper into the structure. 

"I- I guess, yeah." Oceanic stumbled a bit as they caught up. Walking through the structure, the architecture was strange and confusing. *Yarrow seems to navigate it well.*

"Do you like it out here? The land I mean." Yarrow asked.

"It's... different." A silence went on a bit too long. *Say something...* "Uhm... More food up here." They added.

Yarrow let out a small giggle. "Yeah, there's a lot more food." a moment of silence passed. "Speaking of food, there's some right up there!" Yarrow started running ahead. Oceanic jumped a bit at their sudden movement. After what felt like too long, they decided to catch up, running toward Yarrow to close the distance.

Yarrow suddenly stopped, which caused Oceanic to crash into him from behind. Oceanic fell to the ground.

"Oh- You okay?" Yarrow helped them up. *Too close...* Oceanic stepped away a bit when they got up.

"Look, some blue fruit!" Yarrow pointed up. It seemed a bit too far to grab from jumping alone. "Do you think you could help me up and then I could grab it for us?"

"I- uhm... sure?" A bit unsure, they still complied. Oceanic held onto Yarrow and helped him up. He stood on their shoulders and was quite heavy. "H-hurry up-" Oceanic exclaimed. 

"Almost... done!" Yarrow hopped off with a handful of blue fruit. They handed a few to Oceanic and started eating.

Oceanic followed suit and started enjoying the meal. They sat down. Yarrow sat beside them. "That was fun. Most fun I've had in a while, actually." A little giggle slipped out of Yarrow. 

"Yeah..." Oceanic responded. They both continued enjoying the meal.

Yarrow stretched after eating, rolling his shoulders. "Slept well at least. Better than I have in a while, actually."

A small smile. Then they were moving again.

"We should probably get moving!" Yarrow said. Yarrow got up and started moving deeper into the building. Oceanic followed suit. Reaching a pipe, Yarrow entered it. *They seemed so confident. Everything seemed so easy for them... it's annoying.* Oceanic entered it too. Coming out the other side, they saw a big drop with a pool of water below it.

"Guess we have to jump down. Are you afraid of heights?" Yarrow asked jokingly. 

"No."

They watched as Yarrow jumped down into the water. They walked to the edge and looked down at Yarrow far below. Taking a deep breath, they jumped off the ledge, falling down. Adrenaline rushed through their body as they went faster and faster, until **plunk!** They dropped into the water.

Oceanic surfaced and looked at Yarrow, who was already out.

"Knew you could do it!" Yarrow looked back at Oceanic, who was still in the water. "Come on! Let's keep exploring." Yarrow beckoned Oceanic.

They got out of the water and followed Yarrow. Going into another pipe, they entered a cramped, long room. Slowly crawling to the end and entering another pipe, they entered a big open room with a shelter at the end of it.

"Wow, big room.." Oceanic said quietly. 

"Yeah!" Yarrow responded.

"Do you want to go to the shelter right away, or explore a bit?" Yarrow asked. 

"We should probably enter the shelter now... we can explore after." Oceanic responded.

"Fair enough!" Yarrow started heading to the shelter, and Oceanic followed. They passed over some rocks and climbed a pile of rubble. Heading up a pole, they reached the shelter pipe. Entering it, the shelter seemed bigger than the last one. *Lots of room!* They both got comfortable on their sides of the shelter as the door closed.

"Today was fun, Oceanic. I had fun." Yarrow said. 

"... I did too." They both drifted off to sleep.

Waking up as the door loudly opened, Oceanic felt a warmth across their body. Eyes blinking open, they saw Yarrow very close, basically touching. Panicked, Oceanic scrambled away to the corner of the shelter, waking Yarrow.

"W-why were you so close..?" Oceanic asked. 

"Oh." He giggled a bit. "Must have drifted together during the night, my bad!" Yarrow laughed it off. "Why, did you like it~?" Yarrow teased jokingly.

"No. I was just confused about why you were so close..." Oceanic responded.

"If you say so." Yarrow laid back down, and Oceanic calmed down a bit. *What what what what.*
